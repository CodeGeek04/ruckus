# Ruckus

## Register

product

The interface serves the game. Nobody admires the screen, they shout at it. Every design choice is judged by whether it makes the room louder.

## Product purpose

A pack of party games played the Jackbox way: one shared screen, everyone else on their phone. Built in 24 hours for a challenge whose only criteria are that the game is enjoyable and that everyone can play.

Three games ship:

- **Hearsay**: the room secretly answers a question about you. You never hear the question. You have to work out what you were accused of from the votes alone.
- **Who Said It**: real messages from the group's own WhatsApp history. Guess who typed it.
- **Broken Telephone**: a sentence becomes an AI image becomes a sentence, six people deep, then the whole chain is revealed.

## Users

A specific friend group in Delhi and Gurgaon, in their twenties, most of them engineers. They are in a Discord voice call, not a room. One person shares a laptop screen. Everyone else is on a phone.

They know each other extremely well. They roast each other constantly. Most of the humour is reputational: who is always late, who never pays, who moved to Bangalore. They type in Hinglish.

The people judging the game are the people playing it.

## The two surfaces

**The host screen** is watched through Discord's video encoder: soft, compressed, roughly a second behind. It is glanced at from across a room while people are talking over each other. It is for drama only: timers, reveals, scoreboards, huge type, motion. Nothing a player must read in order to act may live only here.

**The phone** is held in one hand, six inches from the face, in a lit room. It does exactly one thing per screen. It is where anything private or actionable lives.

They are the same product and must look it, but they are not the same design problem.

## Tone

Dry, confident, a little mean. The game is a straight-faced institution running an absurd process: Hearsay is a courtroom, Broken Telephone is a machine that misunderstands you on purpose. It never explains its own jokes and never uses an exclamation mark to signal that something was funny.

Commentary fires on genuinely notable events, not every round. A unanimous vote, a total miss, a comeback. Earned, not constant.

## Strategic principles

1. **Legibility beats beauty on the host.** If it does not survive a compressed stream at four metres, it is wrong, however good it looks in a screenshot.
2. **The phone never makes you think.** One action, one screen, thumb-sized targets.
3. **Nothing is ever cut off.** The host screen is a fixed frame, never a scroll. Content that does not fit is a bug.
4. **Personality never touches mechanics.** Easter eggs and commentary are presentation only. They cannot change scores, phases, or what anybody sees that matters.
5. **Everybody plays every round.** No elimination, no dead time, no waiting your turn.

## Anti-references

- **Kahoot.** Primary-colour, classroom, cheerful. Ruckus is not for children and does not congratulate you.
- **Generic dark SaaS.** Slate greys, thin borders, subtle shadows, tasteful restraint. This is a party game, not an observability dashboard.
- **Neon-on-black gamer aesthetic.** Glows, gradients, cyberpunk. Cheap and instantly dated.
- **Corporate illustration.** No blobby figures, no mascots.
- Anything that would make a group of engineers say "this looks AI generated".
